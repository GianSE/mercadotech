Formal employment in Information Technology (IT) in Brazil has grown rapidly over the past two decades, yet the available analyses of the sector typically rely on closed aggregates or short time windows, and cannot be reproduced. This work built a complete analytical platform over the microdata of the Annual Social Information Report (RAIS) and the General Register of Employed and Unemployed Workers (CAGED), covering 2007 to 2026, organized as a Medallion architecture (Bronze, Silver and Gold) that runs entirely in the cloud: the data lake lives in public Hugging Face Hub repositories, and processing is performed by GitHub Actions workflows chained into a dependency graph, which periodically checks the public server of the Ministry of Labour and Employment and reprocesses only the years with new data. Development followed CRISP-DM, whose cycle led to new sources, a revised scope and reformulated questions. The Apache Parquet files are queried by the in-process engine DuckDB with no prior import and presented through a React web dashboard, published as a static site, in which DuckDB-Wasm runs the queries in the visitor's browser. A total of 661~GB of raw microdata in text form was ingested and stored as 56.7~GB of Parquet; the technology scope, defined as the union of economic activity (CNAE) and occupation (CBO), yielded 23.8 million RAIS employment records and 12.6 million CAGED movements. Reproducibility was verified by recounting the scope in the raw data for every year of RAIS and CAGED, which exposed and corrected a duplicate in 2007 and ended with zero difference, and the totals of the occupation lens fell within about 1\% of those in a public panel published by the Ministry. The audit also found the civil service of the state of Rondônia declared as systems programmers in eight years of the series, a defect whose correction changed the sector's growth rate and reversed the trend of the gender gap. The results show that the stock of formal IT jobs rose from 519,271 in 2007 to 1,361,231 in 2025, a 162\% increase, or 5.5\% a year, in waves, with 38\% of the gain concentrated in 2021 and 2022; that 58\% of IT professionals work outside IT companies, down from 71\% in 2007, so that growth was concentrated in IT companies themselves; that median pay measured in minimum wages remained stable (3.39 to 3.41); that the racial wage gap narrowed, through better access, while the gender gap emerged after 2015 and reached 7.8\%, entirely unexplained by observable characteristics; and that a nowcasting estimator for the employment stock, validated by holding out each year from 2014 to 2025, was off by 1.1\% on average, against 5.1\% for the naive estimator. For the monthly balance, the SARIMA model selected through rolling-origin validation outperformed the seasonal naive baseline by 33\% in mean absolute error, against 8.3\% for the best Prophet configuration. Because the data lake is public, the processed layers are also open datasets that third parties can reuse.\\

\textbf{Keywords}: IT labor market; data engineering; RAIS; CAGED; open data.
